# Understanding the Results

This guide explains how to read each chart generated by the project, what patterns to look for, and important limitations to keep in mind before drawing conclusions.

---

## How to Read Each Chart

### 1. Catalog Growth Over Time (`01_growth_trajectory.png`)
- **Chart Type**: Stacked Area Chart.
- **How to read it**: The horizontal axis shows the year Netflix added titles (`Year_Added`). The blue area is **Movies**, and the orange-red area stacked on top is **TV Shows**. The top edge of the combined shape shows total additions per year.
- **What to look for**: Notice how rapidly additions climbed after 2015, and whether TV Shows grew as a share of total additions (reflecting Netflix's shift toward serialized, binge-worthy series).

### 2. Top Content-Producing Countries (`02_top_countries.png`)
- **Chart Type**: Horizontal Bar Chart.
- **How to read it**: Longer bars mean more titles credited to that country. Co-productions (like `"United States, United Kingdom"`) give credit to both countries. Titles with `"Unknown"` country are excluded so they don't hide real production hubs.
- **What to look for**: The United States typically leads, followed by major international hubs such as India, the United Kingdom, South Korea, and Japan.

### 3. Country × Genre Heatmap (`03_country_genre_heatmap.png`)
- **Chart Type**: Annotated Heatmap.
- **How to read it**: Rows are top countries, columns are top genres, and each square's number and color darkness show how many titles match that pair.
- **What to look for**: Regional specialties—for example, India's strength in International Movies/Dramas or South Korea's strength in International/Korean TV Shows.

### 4. Audience Maturity Profile Across Decades (`04_ratings_by_decade.png`)
- **Chart Type**: 100% Stacked Bar Chart.
- **How to read it**: Each bar represents a release decade and adds up to **100%**, making it easy to compare proportions even when older decades had fewer titles.
- **What to look for**: How the share of **Adults** (`TV-MA`, `R`, `NC-17`) vs. **Teens** (`PG-13`, `TV-14`) and **Kids** (`TV-Y`, `TV-Y7`, `G`, `TV-G`) changes in newer decades.

### 5. Extras (`05`–`08`)
- **Movie Runtimes (`05_extra_movie_duration_dist.png`)**: A histogram with a dashed vertical line at the **median** runtime—shows the typical movie length and any very short or very long outliers.
- **Top Directors & Cast (`06_extra_top_directors_cast.png`)**: Highlights the most frequently credited creators (excluding `"Unknown"`).
- **Monthly Seasonality (`07_extra_monthly_seasonality.png`)**: Shows which calendar months saw the most catalog additions.
- **Top Description Terms (`08_extra_description_top_terms.png`)**: Counts the most frequent meaningful words in plot summaries (after removing filler words like *"the"* and *"and"*).

## Important Limitations & Caveats

1. **Catalog ≠ Viewership**: Knowing that Netflix *offers* 1,000 dramas does not tell us how many people *watched* them. This dataset has no view counts, watch hours, or ratings scores.
2. **Snapshot Ends Around September 2021**: Any titles added after late 2021 are not in the Kaggle dataset, and titles that later left Netflix may still appear here.
3. **Sample Mode vs. Full Dataset**: If you haven't added `data/raw/netflix_titles.csv`, the pipeline runs on the 300-row sample (`data/sample/netflix_titles_sample.csv`). Always check the banner at the top of `reports/findings.md` or the Streamlit dashboard to know which dataset you are viewing.
4. **Co-Production Counting**: Because we explode multi-country and multi-genre labels, summing the bars in `02_top_countries.png` will exceed the total number of unique titles—that is expected and intentional so co-productions aren't undercounted.
